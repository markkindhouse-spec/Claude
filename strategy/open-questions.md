- **Direction confirmed by Mark: this is a roll-up.** Sequencing named:
  LB Civil (leg 1, likely the existing heavy civil acquisition), West
  Coast Drilling (leg 2, proposed), a third leg TBD where the platform

  lever is it actually serving** — cross-selling, combined bonding
  capacity for bigger bids, or geographic footprint? Not yet named.
  Candidates raised: earthmoving/mass grading fleet (also feeds Thread 2),
  paving/asphalt, underground utility. Unanswered.
- **SDVOSB/DVBE control conflict — flagged by Mark, not yet resolved.**
  Both certifications require the service-disabled veteran to hold 51%+
  ownership *and* unconditional control (no supermajority vetoes,
  automatic buyout triggers, etc. held by non-veteran owners). A standard
  PE equity/governance structure is very likely incompatible with keeping
  a specific subsidiary's veteran certification alive. This needs a real
  structuring answer (e.g. is BDX's stake non-controlling at the
  certified-entity level, held at a holdco above it, or is the platform
  accepting loss of that certification on some/all legs?) before it's a
  side issue — Mark called it "nonsense to figure out," which suggests
  it hasn't been structured yet.
- **"Keep it double gated" (re: West Coast Drilling)** — meaning
  unconfirmed. Could be physical/security, or could mean keeping WCD as a
  separately gated legal entity under a holdco rather than merging it into
  LB Civil (relevant to licensing, bonding, or the certification question
  above). Ask Mark to clarify plainly.
- **Is there a repeatable acquisition playbook yet** (target profile,
  valuation discipline, 100-day integration plan), or has each deal
  including the heavy civil acquisition been evaluated on its own terms?
  Still unanswered. If BDX's own organizational background turns out to
  be outside heavy civil / infrastructure (worth confirming directly), a
  repeatable playbook is more likely to have to come from Mark's side
  than theirs.
- **What is BDX's return actually underwritten on** — partially answered
  (it's a roll-up), but the specific lever (multiple arbitrage vs.
  operational differentiation vs. vertical integration) is still open.
- Not yet asked: actual binding growth constraint (bonding capacity,
  estimating throughput, super bench depth, operator availability),
  expected hold period / exit mechanism, and whether BDX is structured as
  a committed fund or more flexible capital — changes how much hold-period
  pressure is actually real versus assumed.
